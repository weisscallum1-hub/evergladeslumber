Marine plywood in Miami: what “marine” means and when you need it

People ask for marine plywood any time something will be wet. The label means less than most buyers assume, and it is often more than the job needs.

By Callum Weiss · Updated Nov 5, 2026

What “marine” actually promises

Marine plywood is made with waterproof glue and good-quality veneers, with no voids in the core. That makes it stable and less likely to delaminate when it gets wet. It does not mean the wood itself is rot-proof or termite-proof. Left bare and wet, it can still decay, so edges and faces need sealing.

Different standards exist, and “marine” on a yard’s price list can mean different things. Ask which standard the panel is made to, and what species and grade it is.

Miami conditions

Humidity, rain, salt air near the coast and strong sun are hard on any exterior wood. Sealing, coating and keeping edges protected usually matter as much as the panel you buy.

Alternatives to consider

JobOften considered instead of marine
Signs and painted outdoor panelsMDO or HDO (resin-overlaid plywood). Made for paint and signage.
General exterior, above groundExterior-glue sheathing, or pressure-treated plywood rated for above-ground exposure
Boat building or hull workMarine plywood is the right category. Ask for the specific standard.
Interior wet areasExterior-glue plywood, properly sealed

Whether one of these is acceptable for your job depends on the plans, the exposure and who has to sign off. Treat the table as a starting point for the conversation with the yard.

What to put in your quote request

Example: “10 sheets 4×8 marine plywood, 3/4″, for outdoor sign panels, painted. Pickup in Doral. Open to MDO/HDO if the yard recommends it.”

Saying what the panel is for lets the yard suggest a cheaper product that does the job, which you can then compare against the marine quote.

Questions to ask the yard

  • What standard is this marine panel made to, and what species is the face?
  • Is it void-free, and what is the glue type?
  • Is it in stock? Many yards special-order marine panels.
  • What would you use instead for my application, and why?
Check the plans first. This guide is general information, not engineering or code advice. If your job has plans, an engineer, an inspector or a permit, follow what they specify and confirm with them before you order.
